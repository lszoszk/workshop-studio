Briefing checker — Human Rights Council recorded votes

1 · Pick a recorded vote

2 · Briefing text

Press the button to ask the model for a ~250-word briefing. Only the symbol and the title of the selected document are sent — none of the data on this page; the request leaves your browser. The answer appears in the text box below, marked data-source="ai". You can edit it or paste your own text; editing removes the AI marker and nothing is sent anywhere.

3 · Check the claims

The checker finds every checkable claim in the text — the numbers in favour, against and abstaining, the date, the session, and each State said to have voted a certain way — and compares each with the selected recorded vote.

How to read the verdicts. matches = the recorded vote agrees with the claim. wrong = the recorded vote directly contradicts the claim. cannot be checked = this data has no recorded value to compare the claim with (for example a State that is not among the recorded votes for this document, or a session mentioned without a number). “Cannot be checked” is not the same as “wrong”: wrong means the data contradicts the claim; cannot be checked means the data simply does not cover it.

How this is computed: your text is scanned with fixed patterns — numbers before “in favour / against / abstain…”, “X votes to Y with Z abstentions”, “X-Y-Z” tallies, “unanimously”; dates (ISO, “13 October 2023”, “October 13, 2023”); years near words like “adopted” or “resolution”; “Nth session”; and country names followed by a vote phrase (“voted in favour”, “abstained”, …). Each claim is compared only with the selected record: its official totals (yes / no / abstain), the roll call counted from its votes map, its recorded date and session. All counting and matching is done by this page, deterministically — nothing is estimated.
