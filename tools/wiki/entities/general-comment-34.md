# General Comment No. 34 as an authority in the library

**What it is.** General Comment No. 34 (2011) of the Human Rights Committee on article 19 of the ICCPR, document CCPR/C/GC/34, register (b): treaty-body interpretation, not treaty text. [[ccpr-c-gc-34]] reads the document; this page records who in the library relies on it, and for what. It does not mention neurotechnology.

## In the library

**The paragraphs that travel.** On opinion: [General Comment No. 34, para. 9](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p2 "Paragraph 1 of article 19 requires protection of the right to hold opinions without interference. This is a right to which the Covenant permits no exception or restriction") ✓ and [General Comment No. 34, para. 9](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p2 "All forms of opinion are protected, including opinions of a political, scientific, historic, moral or religious nature") ✓. On restricting expression: [General Comment No. 34, para. 21](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p5 "when a State party imposes restrictions on the exercise of freedom of expression, these may not put in jeopardy the right itself. The Committee recalls that the relation between right and restriction and between norm and exception must not be reversed") ✓.

**The Secretary-General (c)** restates para. 9 as "the Human Rights Committee", without naming the General Comment in the text: [A/81/351, para. 23](pin:A/81/351#p8 "According to the Human Rights Committee, this right is one to which the Covenant permits no exception or restriction, and protects all forms of opinion, including political, scientific, historical, moral and religious opinions. Harassment, intimidation or stigmatization of a person on account of opinions held is forbidden") ✓ (footnote 44, on the same PDF page, cites article 19 and General Comment No. 34, with no paragraph number). The step to neurotechnology is the report's, and it sources it to a Special Rapporteur: [A/81/351, para. 23](pin:A/81/351#p8 "Forced neurological interventions, indoctrination programmes or threats of violence designed to compel individuals to form particular opinions or change their opinion would violate this freedom (see A/73/348)") ✓ (A/73/348 is not in the library).

**The AHRC (d)** draws on three paragraphs through its endnotes, naming the Committee but not the General Comment in the text. On the value of expression: [AHRC 2025, p. 19](pin:AHRC-2025#p19 "‘the foundation stone for every free and democratic society’") ✓ (endnote 53, PDF p. 75, cites para. 2; compare [General Comment No. 34, para. 2](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p1 "They constitute the foundation stone for every free and democratic society") ✓). For brain-computer interfaces as a means of expression, endnote 55 cites para. 12. On restrictions: [AHRC 2025, p. 20](pin:AHRC-2025#p20 "the UN Human Rights Committee has highlighted that, when a State party imposes restrictions on the exercise of freedom of expression, these may not put in jeopardy the right itself") ✓ (endnote 61 cites para. 21), applied to access: [AHRC 2025, p. 20](pin:AHRC-2025#p20 "where Government law or policy denies, or places insurmountable barriers to accessing neurotechnology that is essential for some people to freely communicate, this could constitute a restriction on freedom of expression") ✓.

**A correction this General Comment supplies.** The AHRC places "security of the person" in [AHRC 2025, p. 33](pin:AHRC-2025#p33 "art 19.1 ICCPR which provides that everyone has the right to ’hold opinions without interference’") ✓. Para. 9 shows that the phrase belongs to article 19(1); security of person is article 9(1). See [[iccpr-art-9]].

**Not quoted.** No library document quotes [General Comment No. 34, para. 10](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p3 "Any form of effort to coerce the holding or not holding of any opinion is prohibited") ✓, although A/81/351 para. 23 discusses forced interventions on opinion.

**Finding.** No report names General Comment No. 34 in its running text; both cite it in notes. UNODC–INTERPOL, the Futures Lab and the journal articles do not use it.
