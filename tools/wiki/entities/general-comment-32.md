# General Comment No. 32 as an authority in the library

**What it is.** General Comment No. 32 (2007) of the Human Rights Committee on article 14 of the ICCPR, document CCPR/C/GC/32, register (b): treaty-body interpretation, not treaty text. [[ccpr-c-gc-32]] reads the document; this page records who in the library relies on it, and for what. It does not mention neurotechnology.

## In the library

**The paragraph that travels.** [General Comment No. 32, para. 41](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p13 "This safeguard must be understood in terms of the absence of any direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure from the investigating authorities on the accused, with a view to obtaining a confession of guilt") ✓, with the exclusionary rule and the burden of proof: [General Comment No. 32, para. 41](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p13 "statements or confessions obtained in violation of article 7 of the Covenant are excluded from the evidence … the burden is on the State to prove that statements made by the accused have been given of their own free will") ✓. The same rule is stated for every article 14 proceeding in [General Comment No. 32, para. 6](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p2 "no statements or confessions or, in principle, other evidence obtained in violation of this provision may be invoked as evidence in any proceedings covered by article 14") ✓.

**The Secretary-General (c)** restates para. 41 without naming it in the text: [A/81/351, para. 32](pin:A/81/351#p10 "This privilege is an absolute guarantee and any direct or indirect physical or psychological pressure on the accused with a view to obtaining a confession of guilt is prohibited") ✓ (footnote 62, on the same PDF page, cites General Comment No. 32). Two differences from the Committee's text: the word "undue" before "psychological pressure" is gone, and "absolute guarantee" is the report's phrase. The General Comment does not call the privilege absolute; what it says is that [General Comment No. 32, para. 6](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p2 "Deviating from fundamental principles of fair trial, including the presumption of innocence, is prohibited at all times") ✓. For the exclusionary rule the report cites the Convention against Torture, article 15, and General Comment No. 20 (footnote 64, PDF p. 11), not this General Comment: [A/81/351, para. 32](pin:A/81/351#p10 "under the exclusionary rule, statements or other evidence obtained in violation of the prohibition of torture or ill-treatment may not be invoked as evidence in any proceedings") ✓. The application to brain-based lie detection is the report's own.

**The AHRC (d)** uses the same formula twice, and sources it to the Advisory Committee rather than the Human Rights Committee: [AHRC 2025, p. 31](pin:AHRC-2025#p31 "This means there can be no direct or indirect physical or psychological pressure to secure a confession or testimony") ✓ (endnote 145, PDF p. 78, cites A/HRC/57/61, para. 36) and [AHRC 2025, p. 33](pin:AHRC-2025#p33 "Both of these rights require that no direct or indirect physical or psychological pressure be exerted by authorities to secure a confession or testimony") ✓ (endnote 176, PDF p. 79, same citation). "Undue" is dropped here too. See [[a-hrc-57-61]], which is in the inbox.

**General Comment No. 34 (b)** is the only library document that names General Comment No. 32 in its text: [General Comment No. 34, para. 18](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p4 "The Committee, in general comment No. 32 on article 14, set out the various entitlements to information that are held by those accused of a criminal offence") ✓.

**Finding.** The Committee's formula on compulsion appears in two reports, three times, and neither report names its source in the text; both drop "undue". UNODC–INTERPOL, the Futures Lab and the journal articles name the privilege but not the General Comment. For the Committee's words, pin para. 41 itself.
