# ICCPR article 9 (liberty and security of person)

**What it is.** Article 9(1) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966), register (a), binding treaty text: "Everyone has the right to liberty and security of person. No one shall be subjected to arbitrary arrest or detention. No one shall be deprived of his liberty except on such grounds and in accordance with such procedure as are established by law." The article's later paragraphs cover arrest, judicial control and review of detention. Source: [ICCPR art. 9](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights), (a) treaty text, not in the library.

## In the library

**The Human Rights Committee (b).** The library holds no General Comment on article 9. The Committee's reading, General Comment No. 35 (2014), is cited by A/81/351 (footnotes 66 and 67, PDF p. 11) and by the AHRC (endnote 169), but it is not held. General Comment No. 32 names article 9 only in passing: [General Comment No. 32, para. 31](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p9 "Notice of the reasons for an arrest is separately guaranteed in article 9, paragraph 2 of the Covenant") ✓, and on delay, [General Comment No. 32, para. 61](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p17 "If someone suspected of a crime and detained on the basis of article 9 of the Covenant is charged with an offence but not brought to trial") ✓ both articles 9(3) and 14(3)(c) may be violated.

**The Secretary-General (c).** The one developed treatment in the library: [A/81/351, para. 34](pin:A/81/351#p11 "Neurotechnologies claiming to assess the risk of recidivism, and the use of brain-based deception technologies to determine whether a person should be detained, may lead to arbitrary detention under article 9") ✓. The report glosses the two interests, [A/81/351, para. 34](pin:A/81/351#p11 "liberty of person, meaning freedom from confinement of the body, and security of person, meaning freedom from injury to the body and the mind") ✓ (footnote 66, on the same PDF page, cites General Comments No. 35 and No. 20), states the individuation rule, [A/81/351, para. 34](pin:A/81/351#p11 "the decision to detain, or continue to detain, a person must be based on reasons specific to that individual") ✓, and adds that [A/81/351, para. 34](pin:A/81/351#p11 "Detention based on neural data may also be considered arbitrary, when the right to challenge confinement is undermined by the untransparent and obscure nature of such systems") ✓. See [[neuroprediction]] and [[brain-based-lie-detection]].

**The Australian Human Rights Commission (d): an error to quarantine.** The AHRC writes: [AHRC 2025, p. 33](pin:AHRC-2025#p33 "The protection of the mind and mental integrity is also alluded to in the right to security of the person in art 19.1 ICCPR which provides that everyone has the right to ’hold opinions without interference’. The UN Human Rights Committee notes that security of the person includes ‘freedom of injury to the body and the mind, or bodily or mental integrity’") ✓. Two articles are run together. Security of person is article 9(1); "hold opinions without interference" is article 19(1), as General Comment No. 34 states: [General Comment No. 34, para. 9](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p2 "Paragraph 1 of article 19 requires protection of the right to hold opinions without interference") ✓ (see [[ccpr-c-gc-34]]). The Committee quotation that follows is about article 9: the AHRC's own endnotes 168 and 169 (PDF p. 79) cite "ICCPR art 9.1" and General Comment No. 35, para. 3. Read the sentence with its endnotes, not its body text; the same gloss, correctly placed, is in A/81/351 para. 34 above (the AHRC's quotation also reads "freedom of injury" where A/81/351 has "freedom from injury"). The AHRC invokes article 9 once more, for soldiers coerced into using neurotechnology, [AHRC 2025, p. 49](pin:AHRC-2025#p49 "This may have implications for the right to personal integrity under art 1 UDHR and art 9 ICCPR") ✓, a military setting outside the administration of justice.

**Nothing else.** UNODC–INTERPOL, the Futures Lab submission and the two journal articles do not name article 9. The nearest is UNODC–INTERPOL's list of due process rights, [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 4](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p7 "the right to appear before a competent judicial authority") ✓, with no article number.
