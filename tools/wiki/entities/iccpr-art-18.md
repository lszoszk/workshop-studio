# ICCPR article 18 (freedom of thought, conscience and religion)

**What it is.** Article 18(1) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966), register (a), binding treaty text, opens: "Everyone shall have the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion." Paragraph 2: "No one shall be subject to coercion which would impair his freedom to have or to adopt a religion or belief of his choice." Paragraph 3 allows limitations only on the freedom to manifest a religion or belief. Source: [ICCPR art. 18](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights), (a) treaty text, not in the library. The AHRC reproduces paragraph 1 in a text box [AHRC 2025, p. 28](pin:AHRC-2025#p28 "Everyone shall have the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion") ✓. The library uses the article for the forum internum: thought itself, which paragraph 3 does not reach.

## In the library

**The Human Rights Committee (b).** General Comment No. 22 (1993) is the reading: [General Comment No. 22, para. 1](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p1 "far-reaching and profound; it encompasses freedom of thoughts on all matters") ✓ and [General Comment No. 22, para. 1](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p1 "this provision cannot be derogated from, even in time of public emergency") ✓; [General Comment No. 22, para. 3](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p2 "It does not permit any limitations whatsoever on the freedom of thought and conscience") ✓ and [General Comment No. 22, para. 3](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p2 "In accordance with articles 18 (2) and 17, no one can be compelled to reveal his thoughts or adherence to a religion or belief") ✓; on coercion, [General Comment No. 22, para. 5](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p2 "Article 18 (2) bars coercions that would impair the right to have or adopt a religion or belief, including the use of threat of physical force or penal sanctions") ✓. General Comment No. 34 ties opinion to thought: [General Comment No. 34, para. 5](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p1 "the relationship of opinion and thought (article 18)") ✓. Neither mentions neurotechnology. See [[general-comment-22]] for who relies on these paragraphs.

**The Secretary-General (c).** [A/81/351, para. 20](pin:A/81/351#p7 "Neurotechnologies used to infer mental states, memory or recognition … could reveal information that the person has not chosen to disclose. Coercive neuromodulation technologies can alter brain activity and shape how thoughts occur. Such technologies could thus have a profound impact on the freedom of thought, recognized in article 18") ✓ (footnote 36, on the same PDF page, cites General Comment No. 22, paras. 1 and 3). It takes the "four pillars" of freedom of thought from experts and finds that [A/81/351, para. 22](pin:A/81/351#p8 "Neurotechnology may potentially affect all four pillars of freedom of thought") ✓, and recommends barring neural data obtained [A/81/351, para. 50 (d)](pin:A/81/351#p15 "in violation of the rights to freedom of thought and opinion, as evidence in any proceedings") ✓.

**UNODC and INTERPOL (c).** Listed among the rights to secure: [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 4](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p7 "individuals’ rights to privacy, freedom of thought, bodily integrity, non-discrimination") ✓.

**The Australian Human Rights Commission (d).** [AHRC 2025, p. 28](pin:AHRC-2025#p28 "The right contained in art 18 ICCPR is ‘far reaching and profound’ and captures freedom of thought on all matters and personal convictions") ✓, and [AHRC 2025, p. 28](pin:AHRC-2025#p28 "Freedom of thought is considered part of an individual’s forum internum (internal realm) and is non-derogable") ✓. On coercion it applies paragraph 2 itself: [AHRC 2025, p. 29](pin:AHRC-2025#p29 "Protection against coercion is enshrined in art 18(2) ICCPR … This would protect people from being subject to the nonconsensual use of neurotechnology devices that may alter thought or neural patterns or behaviours") ✓; the Committee has not said this. Its Recommendation 4: [AHRC 2025, p. 70](pin:AHRC-2025#p70 "The United Nations Human Rights Committee provide guidance on freedom of thought and neurotechnologies through the adoption of a general comment on article 18") ✓.

**Civil society (e).** The Futures Lab cites the Universal Declaration: [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "Articles 12 and 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) which protect privacy and freedom of thought, conscience, and religion") ✓.

**Scholarship (f).** Szoszkiewicz and Yuste, without an article number: [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3470](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p2 "intrusions on mental privacy may, in some instances, amount to a violation of the freedom of thought and conscience, an absolute right protected under international human rights law") ✓. Prainsack et al. do not mention it.
