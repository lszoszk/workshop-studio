# California's neural data law

**What it is.** A 2024 amendment to California's consumer privacy statute that adds neural data to its protected personal information. A/81/351 names the bill in footnote 79 (PDF p. 14) as "State of California, Senate Bill No. 1223 (2024)"; AHRC 2025 names the statute amended, the California Consumer Privacy Act 2018; Futures Lab 2026 quotes the definition and, in its footnote 11 (PDF p. 5), dates the bill's approval to 28 September 2024. It is the domestic law of one state of the United States, reported by four library documents of registers (c), (d), (e) and (f). The statute is not in the library, and this page carries the registers of the reports.

## In the library

The Secretary-General's single sentence groups California with Colorado and Montana [A/81/351, para. 45](pin:A/81/351#p14 "California, Colorado and Montana have extended sensitive data protections to neural data") ✓.

The Australian Human Rights Commission names the statute and the technique [AHRC 2025, p. 25](pin:AHRC-2025#p25 "California has now amended the California Consumer Privacy Act 2018 to extend existing consumer protections to an individual’s neural data") ✓.

Futures Lab gives the definition, the one place in the library where a statutory definition of neural data is quoted [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "The California law grants consumers privacy rights regarding personal information and neural data. In this law, neural data is defined as “information that is generated by measuring the activity of a consumer’s central or peripheral nervous system, and that is not inferred from nonneural information.”") ✓.

Szoszkiewicz and Yuste place it with the other two states as an extension of existing consumer legislation [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3471](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p3 "Colorado, California and Montana now explicitly include neurotechnology and neural data in their statutory definitions, and apply to these data existing consumer legislation for personal sensitive data") ✓, and read the voting record as a sign of consensus [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3471](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p3 "all legislative efforts worldwide have been unanimous or nearly unanimous, reflecting emerging bipartisan agreement") ✓.

**The definition against the others.** California's definition, as Futures Lab quotes it, excludes data "inferred from nonneural information". The definition the Secretary-General adopts from UNESCO covers structure as well as activity and is silent on inference [A/81/351, para. 5](pin:A/81/351#p3 "qualitative and quantitative data about the structure, activity and function of the nervous system gathered through neurotechnology") ✓. Szoszkiewicz and Yuste report that legal definitions sometimes go the other way [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3471](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p3 "“Neural data” is generally defined as information generated by measuring activity in the central or peripheral nervous systems, sometimes extending to inferences drawn from that data") ✓. Which definition a State adopts decides whether inferences about a person drawn from other data count as neural data; the library records the options, not a preferred one.

**Where the reports differ.** As for [[colorado-neural-data-law]]: Futures Lab counts California and Colorado as [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "currently the only states to have passed legislation on neural data privacy") ✓, while A/81/351 (para. 45) and Szoszkiewicz and Yuste (p. 3471) add Montana. Prefer the (c) count, dated August 2026.

**What the library does not say.** The text of the Act; what rights "sensitive personal information" status gives a consumer in California; whether the law binds State agencies, prosecutors or courts. Every report places it in the consumer market; nothing in the library applies it to the administration of justice.
