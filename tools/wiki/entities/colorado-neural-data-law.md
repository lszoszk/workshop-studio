# Colorado's neural data law

**What it is.** A 2024 amendment to Colorado's consumer privacy law that brings neural data within the state's category of sensitive data. Two documents name the bill: A/81/351, in footnote 79 (PDF p. 14), as "State of Colorado, House Bill No. 24-1058 (2024)", and AHRC 2025 as House Bill 24-1058; Futures Lab 2026 refers to "the Colorado Privacy Act". It is the domestic law of one state of the United States, reported by four library documents of registers (c), (d), (e) and (f). The statute is not in the library, and this page carries the registers of the reports.

## In the library

The Secretary-General lists Colorado among three states, against the absence of a federal law [A/81/351, para. 45](pin:A/81/351#p14 "While the United States has no federal neurotechnology-specific laws, California, Colorado and Montana have extended sensitive data protections to neural data") ✓. Footnote 79, on the same PDF page, gives the three bills: Colorado House Bill No. 24-1058 (2024), California Senate Bill No. 1223 (2024) and Montana Senate Bill No. 163 (2025).

The Australian Human Rights Commission cites it as an overseas model [AHRC 2025, p. 25](pin:AHRC-2025#p25 "in the US, Colorado signed House Bill 24-1058 to define and protect neural data") ✓.

Futures Lab is the only document to describe what the law covers, and quotes its definition [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "The Colorado Privacy Act regulates “biological, genetic, biochemical, physiological, or neural properties, compositions, or activities or of an individual’s body or bodily function” as sensitive data that is to be protected from business use") ✓. Its footnote 12, on the same PDF page, dates the bill: approved 17 April 2024. The submission's point is the absence of a national rule [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "California and Colorado demonstrate that there is no national standard on which biological data qualifies for privacy protections") ✓, reached by revising an existing law [Futures Lab 2026, p. 7](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p8 "States such as California and Colorado have revised their existing privacy laws and added neural data protections into their legislation") ✓.

Szoszkiewicz and Yuste describe the same technique, a definition added to existing consumer legislation [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3471](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p3 "Colorado, California and Montana now explicitly include neurotechnology and neural data in their statutory definitions, and apply to these data existing consumer legislation for personal sensitive data") ✓.

**Where the reports differ: how many states.** Futures Lab says two [Futures Lab 2026, p. 4](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p5 "California and Colorado are currently the only states to have passed legislation on neural data privacy, while a dozen others have legislation pending") ✓; A/81/351 (para. 45) and Szoszkiewicz and Yuste (p. 3471) say three, adding Montana, whose bill A/81/351 dates to 2025. Futures Lab itself says something larger four pages on [Futures Lab 2026, p. 8](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p9 "At least eight U.S. states have enacted legislation to protect neurorights, addressing concerns about cognitive liberty and biometric data") ✓, citing an advocacy web page (its footnote 40, PDF p. 9). The counts cannot be reconciled from the library: "neural data privacy" and "neurorights" may be counted differently, and the submission's two statements sit uneasily together. Prefer the (c) count, dated August 2026, and cite the bills footnote 79 names.

**What the library does not say.** The text of the Act; what "sensitive data" protection requires in Colorado (consent, limits on sale, enforcement); whether the law reaches State actors, courts or law enforcement, since every report places it in the consumer market. Nothing in the library applies it to the administration of justice.
