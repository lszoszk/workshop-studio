# UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology (2025)

**What it is.** A recommendation adopted by the General Conference of UNESCO in 2025, as the Secretary-General reports [A/81/351, para. 46](pin:A/81/351#p14 "At the international level, the General Conference of UNESCO adopted its Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology in 2025, which calls for national coordinating bodies for neurotechnology, among other measures") ✓. Footnote 4 of the same report (PDF p. 2, on para. 4) dates the adoption: 11 November 2025. It is UN agency soft law, register (c). Its text is not in the library. The wiki knows it through four documents, which saw it at different stages; read each with its date.

## In the library, by date

**Szoszkiewicz and Yuste, July 2025: a draft under negotiation.** [Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025, p. 3471](pin:Szoszkiewicz-Yuste-2025#p3 "UNESCO published a draft instrument in 2024, currently under intergovernmental negotiation") ✓, listed among the soft-law instruments then taking shape.

**AHRC 2025, October 2025: a draft, adoption expected.** [AHRC 2025, p. 9](pin:AHRC-2025#p9 "in 2024 it released its draft recommendation. It is expected that the recommendation will be adopted in late 2025") ✓. The Commission builds on the draft all the same: it takes its definition of neurotechnology from UNESCO [AHRC 2025, p. 8](pin:AHRC-2025#p8 "the Commission has adopted the definition set out by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) as a leading voice on the international application of neurotechnology") ✓, and relies on the draft for a worker's right of access to neural data [AHRC 2025, p. 42](pin:AHRC-2025#p42 "As noted in UNESCO’s draft recommendation on neurotechnology, workers should have a right to obtain access to comprehensible neural data collected about themselves") ✓. Its endnotes (PDF pp. 74, 76, 82 and 86, not in the passages) cite three successive texts: working documents of 17 April 2024 and 27 August 2024, and a "Draft Text of the Recommendation" of 9 April 2025.

**Futures Lab, March 2026: still "draft".** Though submitted after the adoption date A/81/351 gives, the submission speaks of a draft [Futures Lab 2026, p. 5](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p6 "Draft global standards on the ethics of neurotechnology underline that technologies must be developed and used in ways that avoid segregation, discrimination, or subordination of peoples") ✓ and [Futures Lab 2026, p. 5](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p6 "The UNESCO draft calls for gender-responsive policies to close gaps in access and leadership in neurotechnology") ✓. Its footnotes 19 and 20 (PDF p. 6) cite a 2023 MIT Media Lab article and a "Draft Global Standard" of 2023: its source predates the 2024 drafts the other documents cite. Elsewhere it counts UNESCO as guidance without a binding framework [Futures Lab 2026, p. 2](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p3 "there remains no unified international framework for neurorights, despite early leadership from countries such as Chile and guidance from organizations such as UNESCO") ✓.

**A/81/351, August 2026: adopted, and used.** Besides para. 46, the report takes both of its working definitions from the Recommendation: neurotechnology [A/81/351, para. 4](pin:A/81/351#p2 "devices, systems and procedures – encompassing both hardware and software – that directly measure, access, monitor, analyse, predict or modulate the nervous system") ✓ (footnote 4, same PDF page) and neural data [A/81/351, para. 5](pin:A/81/351#p3 "qualitative and quantitative data about the structure, activity and function of the nervous system gathered through neurotechnology") ✓ (footnote 6, same PDF page). It cites the Recommendation (footnote 65, PDF p. 11) for the standard that neurotechnology-based evidence admitted in court [A/81/351, para. 33](pin:A/81/351#p11 "meets the highest standards of scientific validation, transparency and contestability") ✓.

**The time difference, flagged.** Three documents describe a draft (July 2025, October 2025, March 2026); one describes an adopted instrument (August 2026) and dates adoption to 11 November 2025. The AHRC's expectation and the Secretary-General's report agree with each other. Futures Lab's "draft", written four months after that date on 2023 sources, is the one to handle with care: quote it for what a draft said, not for what the Recommendation says. On the definition of neurotechnology the stage makes no difference: the words the AHRC quotes from the draft [AHRC 2025, p. 8](pin:AHRC-2025#p8 "devices, systems, and procedures— encompassing both hardware and software—that directly measure, access, monitor, analyse, predict or modulate the nervous system") ✓ are those A/81/351 quotes from the adopted text.

**What the library does not say.** The Recommendation's structure or provisions beyond the two definitions, the call for national coordinating bodies, the gender-responsive policies and the evidence standard; whether it addresses the administration of justice directly.
