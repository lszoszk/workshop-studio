# ICCPR article 7 (torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment)

**What it is.** Article 7 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966), register (a), binding treaty text: "No one shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. In particular, no one shall be subjected without his free consent to medical or scientific experimentation." Source: [ICCPR art. 7](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights), (a) treaty text, not in the library. The AHRC reproduces the words in a text box [AHRC 2025, p. 33](pin:AHRC-2025#p33 "no one shall be subjected without his free consent to medical or scientific experimentation") ✓. Two limbs matter here: the prohibition of ill-treatment, and free consent to experimentation.

## In the library

**The Human Rights Committee (b).** No General Comment on article 7 is in the library; the reports' footnotes cite General Comment No. 20 (1992), which is not held. General Comment No. 32, on article 14, reads article 7 into the law of evidence: [General Comment No. 32, para. 6](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p2 "as article 7 is also non-derogable in its entirety, no statements or confessions or, in principle, other evidence obtained in violation of this provision may be invoked as evidence in any proceedings covered by article 14") ✓, and where exclusion is claimed [General Comment No. 32, para. 41](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p13 "the burden is on the State to prove that statements made by the accused have been given of their own free will") ✓. The Committee says nothing about neurotechnology; every application below is the report's own.

**The Secretary-General (c).** The first limb, applied to investigation and custody: [A/81/351, para. 28](pin:A/81/351#p9 "brain-based lie detection, decoding … coercive interrogation techniques, and neuromodulation, applied in detention or investigative contexts, may result in mental or physical suffering contravening the protection provided under articles 7 and 10 (1)") ✓ (its footnote 53, on the same PDF page, cites General Comment No. 20). Neuromodulation of offenders, reported in the Advisory Committee's own verb: [A/81/351, para. 29](pin:A/81/351#p10 "may constitute treatment contrary to articles 7 or 10 of the Covenant when imposed without genuinely free and informed consent") ✓ (the view is attributed to [[a-hrc-57-61]], which is in the inbox and is not quoted here). The second limb: [A/81/351, para. 29](pin:A/81/351#p10 "Article 7 of the Covenant “expressly prohibits medical or scientific experimentation without the free consent of the person concerned”") ✓ (footnote 57 cites General Comment No. 20, para. 7). Evidence: [A/81/351, para. 32](pin:A/81/351#p10 "under the exclusionary rule, statements or other evidence obtained in violation of the prohibition of torture or ill-treatment may not be invoked as evidence in any proceedings. This could apply to evidence obtained through the coercive use of neurotechnologies") ✓. The report recommends that States [A/81/351, para. 50 (d)](pin:A/81/351#p15 "Prohibit the use of neurotechnology as a means of torture or other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment") ✓.

**UNODC and INTERPOL (c).** The paper lists [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 4](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p7 "the prohibition of torture, inhuman and degrading treatment") ✓ among the rights institutions must secure, and names the coercive offer: [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 4](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p7 "“offered” the use of these technologies in ways that are coercive, or under the guise of moral-enhancement") ✓.

**The Australian Human Rights Commission (d).** On memory recovery: [AHRC 2025, p. 33](pin:AHRC-2025#p33 "if neurotechnology is forcibly used to extract information from suspects they may breach the prohibition of torture, cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. The UN Human Rights Advisory Committee has observed that any misuse of the technology to this degree would represent a violation of art 7 ICCPR") ✓. The body it calls "the UN Human Rights Advisory Committee" is the Human Rights Council Advisory Committee ([[a-hrc-57-61]]); its endnote 170 (PDF p. 79) cites A/HRC/57/61, paras. 34–35.

**Civil society (e) and scholarship (f).** The Futures Lab submission and the two journal articles do not invoke article 7.
