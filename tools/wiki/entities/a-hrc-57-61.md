## In the library

**A/81/351 (c)** relies on it more than on any other UN text: five paragraphs cite it by symbol, and nine footnotes. In the text, the Secretary-General attributes to the Committee a view on neuromodulation of offenders [A/81/351, para. 29](pin:A/81/351#p10 "The Human Rights Council Advisory Committee is of the view that the use of neuromodulation techniques intended to alter the brain activity of offenders to reduce aggression or impulsivity may constitute treatment contrary to articles 7 or 10 of the Covenant when imposed without genuinely free and informed consent") ✓; a finding on who is most exposed [A/81/351, para. 37](pin:A/81/351#p12 "The Human Rights Council Advisory Committee has identified “groups experiencing structural discrimination and marginalized people or those in positions of socioeconomic disadvantage” as being particularly vulnerable to negative impacts of the use of neurotechnology in the administration of justice") ✓ (citing its para. 37); a phrase on remedies [A/81/351, para. 36](pin:A/81/351#p12 "potentially rendering the right to a remedy “illusory, inaccessible or ineffective” and raising accountability issues") ✓ (citing its para. 72); a statement of what the technology can do [A/81/351, para. 22](pin:A/81/351#p8 "While neurotechnology is not capable at present of “mind-reading”, it is reportedly capable of inferring emotions and internal reactions based on neural data") ✓; and an assessment of national regulation [A/81/351, para. 42](pin:A/81/351#p13 "At the national level, the regulatory response to the human rights challenges posed by neurotechnology is still nascent and uneven") ✓.

## What to check at ingest

Each claim above is a second-hand attribution. Against the text, verify:

1. Para. 37 (per A/81/351): the quoted phrase on structurally discriminated and marginalized groups, and whether UNODC–INTERPOL's "p. 37" is the same paragraph.
2. Para. 72 (per A/81/351): the phrase "illusory, inaccessible or ineffective" on remedies.
3. The view that neuromodulation of offenders without genuinely free and informed consent "may constitute" treatment contrary to articles 7 or 10 (A/81/351 para. 29): find the paragraph and the Committee's own verb.
4. Paras. 34–35 (per AHRC endnote 170): whether forcible extraction of information "would represent a violation of art 7" is the Committee's wording or the AHRC's strengthening of it.
5. The "not capable at present of mind-reading" but "inferring emotions" statement (A/81/351 para. 22).
6. The recommendation of General Comments on freedom of thought and on mental integrity for persons with disabilities (Szoszkiewicz and Yuste): to which bodies, in what terms.
7. The "nascent and uneven" assessment of national regulation (A/81/351 para. 42), and what the report says of the United States, Chile and others.
8. Whether the report uses "neurodata" (UNODC–INTERPOL's footnote) and how it defines it, against the UNESCO definition A/81/351 adopts.
9. The date: 8 August 2024 (metadata, AHRC endnotes, UNODC–INTERPOL footnotes) against the AHRC's "released in September 2024".
10. Whether the report takes a side on new rights versus existing rights (A/81/351 footnote 29 cites it on that debate), and what it says of Selvi (footnote 55).
