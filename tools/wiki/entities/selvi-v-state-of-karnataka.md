# Selvi v. State of Karnataka (Supreme Court of India, 2010)

**What it is.** A judgment of the Supreme Court of India on brain-based lie detection, which A/81/351 cites in its footnotes as "Supreme Court of India, Selvi v. State of Karnataka, Judgment, 5 May 2010" (footnote 17, PDF p. 4; footnote 63, PDF p. 11, adds "paras. 215–224"). It is domestic law. The judgment is not in the library: the wiki knows it only through the Secretary-General's report, a (c) document, and this page carries that register. Nothing here is a reading of the judgment itself.

## In the library

Only A/81/351 reports the case: twice in the text, three times in footnotes.

In its survey of brain-based deception detection, the Secretary-General reports the holding and its evidentiary consequence [A/81/351, para. 9](pin:A/81/351#p4 "The Supreme Court of India held that the involuntary administration of brain electrical oscillation signature profiling tests violates the constitutional protection against self-incrimination and renders the resulting evidence inadmissible") ✓ (its footnote 17, on the same PDF page, gives the date). The same paragraph adds that the ruling did not end the practice [A/81/351, para. 9](pin:A/81/351#p4 "However, voluntary use appears to have continued in practice") ✓.

In the fair-trial section, the case is placed next to article 14 (3) (g) of the Covenant [A/81/351, para. 32](pin:A/81/351#p10 "The Supreme Court of India, in Selvi v. State of Karnataka, held that the involuntary administration of brain electrical oscillation signature profiling tests, a form of brain-based lie detection, violates the constitutional protection against self-incrimination") ✓. The footnote to that sentence (footnote 63, printed on PDF p. 11) pinpoints paras. 215–224 of the judgment and cites alongside it a 2024 European Parliamentary Research Service study on mental privacy.

A third mention sits in a footnote to the paragraph on torture and ill-treatment. That paragraph says brain-based lie detection applied in detention or investigative contexts [A/81/351, para. 28](pin:A/81/351#p9 "may result in mental or physical suffering contravening the protection provided under articles 7 and 10 (1) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights") ✓; its footnote 55, on the same PDF page, offers Selvi as an instance that "established a landmark precedent by prohibiting coercive neuro-imaging techniques as a form of degrading treatment", adding "See also A/HRC/57/61".

**Two framings in one report.** The text (paras. 9 and 32) presents Selvi as a self-incrimination holding; footnote 55 presents it as a degrading-treatment holding. The library contains no text of the judgment and cannot say which description is the better one, or whether both hold. A reader who needs the ground of decision must open the judgment at the paragraphs footnote 63 names.

**What the library does not say.** The facts of the case; the Court's reasoning; whether the Court addressed consent given under inducement; what brain electrical oscillation signature profiling measures, beyond the report's one-line description of the technique family [A/81/351, para. 8](pin:A/81/351#p4 "These techniques measure physiological or brain responses to determine whether a person recognizes concealed or crime-related information") ✓. No other library document names the case.
