# General Comment No. 32: article 14, equality before courts and fair trial

**Full cite.** Human Rights Committee, General Comment No. 32: Article 14: Right to equality before courts and tribunals and to a fair trial, UN Doc. CCPR/C/GC/32 (2007), 65 paragraphs, [docs.un.org](https://docs.un.org/en/CCPR/C/GC/32). It replaces General Comment No. 13 [General Comment No. 32, para. 1](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p1 "This general comment replaces general comment No. 13") ✓.

**Register.** (b) treaty-body interpretation of article 14: the highest authority in the library on self-incrimination, exclusion of evidence and the presumption of innocence. It outweighs every report that restates it.

**One-liner.** The privilege against self-incrimination means the absence of any direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure to obtain a confession; evidence obtained contrary to article 7 must be excluded; otherwise admissibility is primarily for domestic law.

## Key takeaways

**The self-incrimination standard (para. 41).** The guarantee "must be understood in terms of the absence of any direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure from the investigating authorities on the accused, with a view to obtaining a confession of guilt"; statements obtained in violation of article 7 must be excluded, and the State must then prove they were given freely [General Comment No. 32, para. 41](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p13 "This safeguard must be understood in terms of the absence of any direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure from the investigating authorities on the accused, with a view to obtaining a confession of guilt … Domestic law must ensure that statements or confessions obtained in violation of article 7 of the Covenant are excluded from the evidence … the burden is on the State to prove that statements made by the accused have been given of their own free will") ✓.

**Article 7 and article 14(3)(g) together (para. 60).** Forcing a confession under duress "violates both article 7 of the Covenant prohibiting torture and inhuman, cruel or degrading treatment and article 14, paragraph 3 (g)" [General Comment No. 32, para. 60](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p17 "To ill-treat persons against whom criminal charges are brought and to force them to make or sign, under duress, a confession admitting guilt violates both article 7 of the Covenant prohibiting torture and inhuman, cruel or degrading treatment and article 14, paragraph 3 (g) prohibiting compulsion to testify against oneself or confess guilt") ✓.

**Exclusion even in emergencies (para. 6).** Article 14 is not among the non-derogable rights, but because article 7 is, "no statements or confessions or, in principle, other evidence obtained in violation of this provision may be invoked as evidence in any proceedings covered by article 14, including during a state of emergency"; deviating from fundamental fair-trial principles "is prohibited at all times" [General Comment No. 32, para. 6](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p2 "no statements or confessions or, in principle, other evidence obtained in violation of this provision may be invoked as evidence in any proceedings covered by article 14, including during a state of emergency … Deviating from fundamental principles of fair trial, including the presumption of innocence, is prohibited at all times") ✓.

**Admissibility is otherwise domestic (paras. 39, 26).** Subject to those limits, "it is primarily for the domestic legislatures of States parties to determine the admissibility of evidence and how their courts assess it" [General Comment No. 32, para. 39](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p12 "subject to the limitations on the use of statements, confessions and other evidence obtained in violation of article 7 … it is primarily for the domestic legislatures of States parties to determine the admissibility of evidence and how their courts assess it") ✓; the Committee revisits a domestic evaluation of evidence only where it "was clearly arbitrary or amounted to a manifest error or denial of justice" [General Comment No. 32, para. 26](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p8 "unless it can be shown that such evaluation or application was clearly arbitrary or amounted to a manifest error or denial of justice") ✓.

**Disclosure about how evidence was obtained (para. 33).** Exculpatory material includes "indications that a confession was not voluntary"; where an article 7 violation is claimed, "information about the circumstances in which such evidence was obtained must be made available" [General Comment No. 32, para. 33](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p10 "other evidence that could assist the defence (e.g. indications that a confession was not voluntary). In cases of a claim that evidence was obtained in violation of article 7 of the Covenant, information about the circumstances in which such evidence was obtained must be made available") ✓.

**Presumption of innocence (para. 30).** It "imposes on the prosecution the burden of proving the charge", requires proof "beyond reasonable doubt", and bars presenting defendants "in a manner indicating that they may be dangerous criminals" [General Comment No. 32, para. 30](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p9 "imposes on the prosecution the burden of proving the charge, guarantees that no guilt can be presumed until the charge has been proved beyond reasonable doubt … presented to the court in a manner indicating that they may be dangerous criminals") ✓.

**Fairness and equality.** Fairness "entails the absence of any direct or indirect influence, pressure or intimidation or intrusion from whatever side and for whatever motive" [General Comment No. 32, para. 25](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p7 "Fairness of proceedings entails the absence of any direct or indirect influence, pressure or intimidation or intrusion from whatever side and for whatever motive") ✓. Procedural distinctions on the grounds listed in articles 2(1) or 26 "may also amount to discrimination" [General Comment No. 32, para. 65](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p18 "not only violate the requirement of paragraph 1 of this provision that “all persons shall be equal before the courts and tribunals,” but may also amount to discrimination") ✓.

## How NOT to read this

- **It is from 2007 and never mentions neurotechnology**, lie detection or brain imaging. Reading para. 41 to cover "the coercive use of brain-based lie detection" is the Secretary-General's step ([[a-81-351]], para. 32) and the AHRC's ([[ahrc-2025]], pp. 31–33). Attribute it to them.
- **"Undue" is part of the standard.** The Committee bars "direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure". A/81/351 (para. 32) and the AHRC (pp. 31, 33) restate it as "direct or indirect physical or psychological pressure", without "undue". Quote the Committee.
- **"Absolute" is the report's word.** A/81/351 para. 32 calls the privilege "an absolute guarantee". The Committee does not say so of article 14(3)(g); it uses "absolute" for the competence, independence and impartiality of a tribunal [General Comment No. 32, para. 19](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p5 "is an absolute right that is not subject to any exception") ✓, and para. 6 recalls that article 14 is not listed in article 4(2). The practical result is close; the label differs.
- **The standard is framed around confessions**, by "the investigating authorities". Whether a brain-based test that yields no statement is "testimony" is not decided here. Selvi v. State of Karnataka decided that under the Indian Constitution, not the Covenant ([[selvi-v-state-of-karnataka]]).
- **The exclusionary rule is keyed to article 7**, not to every involuntary or unreliable item. A/81/351 para. 32 says it "could apply" to coercively obtained neurotechnology evidence: keep "could". Reliability as such is left to domestic law (paras. 26, 39): see [[reliability-and-admissibility]].
- **The burden shift** ("the burden is on the State to prove … free will") arises where an article 7 violation is claimed, not in every consent dispute.
