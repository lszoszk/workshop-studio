# General Comment No. 22: freedom of thought, conscience and religion (article 18)

**Full cite.** Human Rights Committee, General Comment No. 22: The right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion (art. 18), UN Doc. CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4 (1993), 11 paragraphs, [docs.un.org](https://docs.un.org/en/CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4).

**Register.** (b) treaty-body interpretation: the Committee's reading of article 18 of the Covenant. It binds no one as such, but it is the highest authority in the library below the treaty text itself, and it outweighs any report that quotes it.

**One-liner.** Freedom of thought and conscience is absolute: no limitation, no derogation, no compulsion to reveal one's thoughts; only the manifestation of religion or belief may be restricted, and only under article 18(3).

## Key takeaways

**Far-reaching, equal, non-derogable.** The right "is far-reaching and profound"; thought and conscience "are protected equally with the freedom of religion and belief"; the provision "cannot be derogated from, even in time of public emergency" [General Comment No. 22, para. 1](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p1 "is far-reaching and profound; it encompasses freedom of thoughts on all matters, personal conviction … the freedom of thought and the freedom of conscience are protected equally with the freedom of religion and belief … this provision cannot be derogated from, even in time of public emergency") ✓.

**No limitation on thought; no compulsion to reveal it.** Article 18 separates the inner freedoms from their manifestation and "does not permit any limitations whatsoever on the freedom of thought and conscience"; these are "protected unconditionally, as is the right of everyone to hold opinions without interference in article 19 (1)"; and, "in accordance with articles 18 (2) and 17, no one can be compelled to reveal his thoughts" [General Comment No. 22, para. 3](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p2 "It does not permit any limitations whatsoever on the freedom of thought and conscience … These freedoms are protected unconditionally, as is the right of everyone to hold opinions without interference in article 19 (1). In accordance with articles 18 (2) and 17, no one can be compelled to reveal his thoughts or adherence to a religion or belief") ✓.

**Coercion.** Article 18(2) "bars coercions that would impair the right to have or adopt a religion or belief", including "the use of threat of physical force or penal sanctions"; policies "having the same intention or effect", such as restricting access to education, medical care or employment, are "similarly inconsistent"; and "the same protection is enjoyed by holders of all beliefs of a non-religious nature" [General Comment No. 22, para. 5](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p2 "Article 18 (2) bars coercions that would impair the right to have or adopt a religion or belief, including the use of threat of physical force or penal sanctions … Policies or practices having the same intention or effect, such as for example those restricting access to education, medical care, employment … The same protection is enjoyed by holders of all beliefs of a non-religious nature") ✓.

**Restrictions reach manifestation only, and strictly.** Article 18(3) permits restrictions "on the freedom to manifest religion or belief only"; freedom from coercion "cannot be restricted"; paragraph 3 "is to be strictly interpreted", with no restriction on grounds not listed, "such as national security"; and prisoners "continue to enjoy their rights to manifest their religion or belief to the fullest extent compatible with the specific nature of the constraint" [General Comment No. 22, para. 8](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p3 "The freedom from coercion to have or to adopt a religion or belief and the liberty of the parents and guardians to ensure religious and moral education cannot be restricted … paragraph 3 of article 18 is to be strictly interpreted: restrictions are not allowed on grounds not specified there, even if they would be allowed as restrictions to other rights protected in the Covenant, such as national security … Persons already subject to certain legitimate constraints, such as prisoners, continue to enjoy their rights to manifest their religion or belief to the fullest extent compatible with the specific nature of the constraint") ✓.

**Official ideology.** Treating a set of beliefs as official ideology "shall not result in any impairment of the freedoms under article 18" or in discrimination against those who do not accept it [General Comment No. 22, para. 10](pin:CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.4#p4 "this shall not result in any impairment of the freedoms under article 18 or any other rights recognized under the Covenant nor in any discrimination against persons who do not accept the official ideology or who oppose it") ✓.

## How NOT to read this

- **It is from 1993 and never mentions neurotechnology**, brains, data or decoding. The step from "no one can be compelled to reveal his thoughts" to brain-based decoding is taken by the Secretary-General ([[a-81-351]], paras. 20–21) and the AHRC ([[ahrc-2025]], pp. 28–29, 39). Attribute the application to them, not to the Committee.
- **"Compelled to reveal" is about compulsion, and the Committee ties it to articles 18(2) and 17 and to "adherence to a religion or belief".** Whether inference from neural data without the person's participation is "compulsion to reveal" is a question the Committee has not addressed. Say so when the wiki uses paragraph 3 for [[brain-based-lie-detection]].
- **Paragraph 5's coercion standard is about having or adopting a belief**, with examples of force, penal sanctions and withheld benefits. Using it for inducements in custody ([[consent-in-custody]]) or for interventions that alter thought ([[neuromodulation-of-offenders]]) is the wiki's analogy; the Committee has said nothing about those settings.
- **Most of the document is about religion and belief** (paras. 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11: manifestation, education, state religion, conscientious objection). The forum internum points are paras. 1, 3, 5 and 8.
- **Wording.** The library text (and the PDF) reads "freedom of thoughts on all matters"; A/81/351 para. 20 quotes it as "freedom of thought on all matters". Pin the library text.
- **No General Comment on neurotechnology exists in the library.** The AHRC recommends that the Committee adopt one on article 18 and neurotechnologies ([[ahrc-2025]], p. 70), and [[szoszkiewicz-yuste-2025]] reports the Advisory Committee's similar recommendation. Those are calls for an instrument, not the instrument.
