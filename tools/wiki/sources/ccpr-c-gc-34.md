# General Comment No. 34: article 19, freedoms of opinion and expression

**Full cite.** Human Rights Committee, General Comment No. 34: Article 19: Freedoms of opinion and expression, UN Doc. CCPR/C/GC/34 (2011), 52 paragraphs, [docs.un.org](https://docs.un.org/en/CCPR/C/GC/34). It replaces General Comment No. 10 [General Comment No. 34, para. 1](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p1 "This general comment replaces general comment No. 10") ✓.

**Register.** (b) treaty-body interpretation of article 19. Most of it concerns expression and the media; the paragraphs that matter here are on holding opinions (paras. 5, 9, 10, 49) and, in passing, on personal data (para. 18).

**One-liner.** Holding an opinion is a right "to which the Covenant permits no exception or restriction"; any effort to coerce the holding or not holding of an opinion is prohibited; freedom to express includes freedom not to express.

## Key takeaways

**No exception, no restriction (para. 9).** Article 19(1) protects "the right to hold opinions without interference", a right "to which the Covenant permits no exception or restriction"; "no person may be subject to the impairment of any rights under the Covenant on the basis of his or her actual, perceived or supposed opinions"; and "arrest, detention, trial or imprisonment for reasons of the opinions they may hold" violates it [General Comment No. 34, para. 9](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p2 "This is a right to which the Covenant permits no exception or restriction … No person may be subject to the impairment of any rights under the Covenant on the basis of his or her actual, perceived or supposed opinions … The harassment, intimidation or stigmatization of a person, including arrest, detention, trial or imprisonment for reasons of the opinions they may hold, constitutes a violation of article 19, paragraph 1") ✓.

**No coercion; freedom not to express (para. 10).** "Any form of effort to coerce the holding or not holding of any opinion is prohibited", and freedom to express "necessarily includes freedom not to express one's opinion" [General Comment No. 34, para. 10](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p3 "Any form of effort to coerce the holding or not holding of any opinion is prohibited … Freedom to express one’s opinion necessarily includes freedom not to express one’s opinion") ✓.

**Opinion and thought; no reservation, no derogation (paras. 5, 49).** Given "the relationship of opinion and thought (article 18)", a reservation to article 19(1) would be incompatible with the Covenant's object and purpose; freedom of opinion cannot be derogated from, "since it can never become necessary to derogate from it during a state of emergency" [General Comment No. 34, para. 5](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p1 "as well as the relationship of opinion and thought (article 18), a reservation to paragraph 1 would be incompatible with the object and purpose of the Covenant … Freedom of opinion is one such element, since it can never become necessary to derogate from it during a state of emergency") ✓. Restrictions on freedom of opinion "should never be imposed" [General Comment No. 34, para. 49](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p12 "Restrictions on the right of freedom of opinion should never be imposed") ✓.

**Neighbouring articles; private actors (paras. 4, 7).** Articles 18, 17, 25 and 27 also "contain guarantees for freedom of opinion and/or expression" [General Comment No. 34, para. 4](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p1 "Among the other articles that contain guarantees for freedom of opinion and/or expression, are articles 18, 17, 25 and 27") ✓. The obligation binds the judiciary too, and States must protect persons "from any acts by private persons or entities that would impair the enjoyment of the freedoms of opinion and expression", so far as those rights apply between private parties [General Comment No. 34, para. 7](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p2 "All branches of the State (executive, legislative and judicial) … to ensure that persons are protected from any acts by private persons or entities that would impair the enjoyment of the freedoms of opinion and expression to the extent that these Covenant rights are amenable to application between private persons or entities") ✓.

**Personal data, restated from General Comment No. 16 (para. 18).** Everyone "should have the right to ascertain in an intelligible form, whether, and if so, what personal data is stored in automatic data files, and for what purposes", and to have incorrect or unlawfully collected data rectified [General Comment No. 34, para. 18](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p4 "every individual should have the right to ascertain in an intelligible form, whether, and if so, what personal data is stored in automatic data files, and for what purposes … If such files contain incorrect personal data or have been collected or processed contrary to the provisions of the law, every individual should have the right to have his or her records rectified") ✓.

**Restrictions apply to expression, not opinion (para. 21).** When a State restricts expression, "the relation between right and restriction and between norm and exception must not be reversed" [General Comment No. 34, para. 21](pin:CCPR/C/GC/34#p5 "these may not put in jeopardy the right itself. The Committee recalls that the relation between right and restriction and between norm and exception must not be reversed") ✓.

## How NOT to read this

- **It is from 2011 and never mentions neurotechnology.** The step from "no coercion to hold or not hold an opinion" to "forced neurological interventions … would violate this freedom" is the Secretary-General's ([[a-81-351]], para. 23, citing A/73/348), not the Committee's.
- **"Perceived or supposed opinions" (para. 9)** was written about ordinary attribution of opinions. Its use for decoded or predicted mental states is the wiki's application.
- **Opinion is not every mental state.** Para. 5 links opinion to thought (article 18) without drawing the boundary. Nothing here says that an emotion, a memory or a recognition signal is an "opinion". For thought, pin [[ccpr-c-21-rev-1-add-4]].
- **This is article 19, not article 9.** "Security of the person" is article 9 of the Covenant. The AHRC (p. 33) places "the right to security of the person" in "art 19.1 ICCPR" and pairs it with "hold opinions without interference"; the Committee's words it then quotes, on injury "to the body and the mind", concern security of person, not opinion. General Comment No. 34 contains no such passage, and the library holds no General Comment on article 9. See [[iccpr-art-9]] and [[ahrc-2025]].
- **Para. 18 is a restatement.** It repeats General Comment No. 16 on article 17, which is not in the library; pin para. 18 for the Committee's words. A/81/351 para. 26 relies on the same rules and adds that they "would also apply to neural data": that extension is the report's.
- **Do not run a proportionality test on opinion.** Paras. 21–36 govern restrictions on expression (article 19(2)–(3)); opinion (19(1)) admits none (paras. 9, 49). The AHRC quotes para. 21 at p. 20 in the context of expression, correctly.
