# Discrimination and neurotechnology

**One-liner.** Every document in the library that touches criminal justice says the same thing in its own register: neurotechnology in the administration of justice will fall hardest on people already over-represented in it and under-represented in the data it is built on. General Comment No. 32 gives the (b) baseline (equality before courts; no procedural distinctions on article 2 (1) or 26 grounds); the Secretary-General names the groups and recommends enhanced safeguards; UNODC–INTERPOL, the AHRC and Futures Lab agree on the mechanism: biased data, tools validated on narrow populations, structural inequality.

## What the library says

### (b) The Human Rights Committee

General Comment No. 32 predates neurotechnology; its rules apply to any evidence or tool. Equality before courts "ensures that the parties to the proceedings in question are treated without any discrimination" [General Comment No. 32, para. 8](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p2 "ensures that the parties to the proceedings in question are treated without any discrimination") ✓. Racial bias in the tribunal is a fairness defect: "Expressions of racist attitudes by a jury … or a racially biased jury selection are other instances which adversely affect the fairness of the procedure" [General Comment No. 32, para. 25](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p7 "Expressions of racist attitudes by a jury … or a racially biased jury selection are other instances which adversely affect the fairness of the procedure") ✓. Procedural rules that distinguish on the grounds in articles 2 (1) or 26 "may also amount to discrimination" [General Comment No. 32, para. 65](pin:CCPR/C/GC/32#p18 "Procedural laws or their application that make distinctions based on any of the criteria listed in article 2, paragraph 1 or article 26 … may also amount to discrimination") ✓.

### (c) The Secretary-General, A/81/351

Non-discrimination is among the rights the report says neurotechnology "may engage", alongside CERD, CEDAW, the CRC and the CRPD [A/81/351, para. 15](pin:A/81/351#p5 "freedom of thought, expression and opinion, non-discrimination and an effective remedy. Other relevant treaties include … the International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination") ✓. The groups are the Advisory Committee's, "groups experiencing structural discrimination and marginalized people or those in positions of socioeconomic disadvantage", and the mechanism is the report's: "Discrimination stems from biased training data, tools validated on limited populations and structural inequalities in criminal justice systems" [A/81/351, para. 37](pin:A/81/351#p12 "groups experiencing structural discrimination and marginalized people or those in positions of socioeconomic disadvantage … Discrimination stems from biased training data, tools validated on limited populations and structural inequalities in criminal justice systems") ✓ (the citation is to A/HRC/57/61, para. 37, the Advisory Committee's report [[a-hrc-57-61]], in the inbox). Women are exposed to "non-representative, male-centric data sets that can distort assessments of veracity or recidivism risk" [A/81/351, para. 37](pin:A/81/351#p12 "non-representative, male-centric data sets that can distort assessments of veracity or recidivism risk") ✓.

"Technology is never neutral": people of African descent and racialized minorities "are disproportionately affected by algorithmic bias in data sets that underrepresent them and by predictive tools that mirror and amplify historical discrimination", Indigenous Peoples and minorities are more exposed "given their disproportionate representation in criminal justice systems", and "Inclusive validation, cultural competence, community-based governance, accountability and the meaningful participation of affected groups are essential preconditions for the lawful deployment of neurotechnology" [A/81/351, para. 39](pin:A/81/351#p12 "disproportionately affected by algorithmic bias in data sets that underrepresent them and by predictive tools that mirror and amplify historical discrimination … given their disproportionate representation in criminal justice systems … Inclusive validation, cultural competence, community-based governance, accountability and the meaningful participation of affected groups are essential preconditions for the lawful deployment of neurotechnology") ✓. Persons with disabilities and neurodivergent people "may experience misinterpretation of atypical neural patterns, pressure towards forced “normalization” and discrimination on the basis of neurological difference" [A/81/351, para. 41](pin:A/81/351#p13 "persons with disabilities and neurodivergent individuals may experience misinterpretation of atypical neural patterns, pressure towards forced “normalization” and discrimination on the basis of neurological difference") ✓. Children's exposure to predictive labelling is on [[neuroprediction]]. The recommendation: "enhanced safeguards for children, detainees, persons with disabilities, women, older persons, persons belonging to particular racial or ethnic groups, including people of African descent, Indigenous Peoples and persons in situations of vulnerability", with consultation of affected groups [A/81/351, para. 50 (g)](pin:A/81/351#p16 "Ensure enhanced safeguards for children, detainees, persons with disabilities, women, older persons, persons belonging to particular racial or ethnic groups, including people of African descent, Indigenous Peoples and persons in situations of vulnerability") ✓, and regulation with "data-testing protocols and thresholds to identify and mitigate risks of algorithmic bias" [A/81/351, para. 50 (a)](pin:A/81/351#p15 "including data-testing protocols and thresholds to identify and mitigate risks of algorithmic bias") ✓.

### (c) UNODC–INTERPOL 2025

The paper relays the same Advisory Committee concern: "the potential for the discriminatory use of neurotechnologies – particularly in relation to groups that have been marginalized, subjected to structural discrimination, or are socioeconomically disadvantaged – is a key concern" [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 4](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p7 "the potential for the discriminatory use of neurotechnologies – particularly in relation to groups that have been marginalized, subjected to structural discrimination, or are socioeconomically disadvantaged – is a key concern") ✓. In prisons, where people with mental health conditions are over-represented, "the neurotechnologies used in this context must not be used to further discriminate against these individuals" [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 2](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p5 "given the vulnerability of this population, the neurotechnologies used in this context must not be used to further discriminate against these individuals") ✓. The safeguard: "Safeguards must also ensure that neurotechnologies are not deployed in ways that reinforce existing inequalities or result in discriminatory impacts on marginalized groups" [UNODC–INTERPOL 2025, p. 6](pin:UNODC-INTERPOL-2025#p9 "Safeguards must also ensure that neurotechnologies are not deployed in ways that reinforce existing inequalities or result in discriminatory impacts on marginalized groups") ✓.

### (d) AHRC 2025

The Commission's principle: "Anti-discrimination law principles should be applied to the development and use of neurotechnology, considering especially the needs of people who are in vulnerable situations" [AHRC 2025, p. 11](pin:AHRC-2025#p11 "Anti-discrimination law principles should be applied to the development and use of neurotechnology, considering especially the needs of people who are in vulnerable situations") ✓. In criminal justice its worry is categorisation by biomarker: "the risk of discrimination and stigmatisation of groups of people because neurotechnology identifies that they have biological markers that indicate a higher risk of criminal activity", which "may breach human rights to be treated equally before the law and to non-discrimination" [AHRC 2025, p. 35](pin:AHRC-2025#p35 "the risk of discrimination and stigmatisation of groups of people because neurotechnology identifies that they have biological markers that indicate a higher risk of criminal activity") ✓. The report coins "neurodiscrimination" for the workplace (Australian law, outside justice): "neurodivergent people may be at heightened risk of neurodiscrimination" and older workers of "age discrimination on the basis of neural data" [AHRC 2025, p. 42](pin:AHRC-2025#p42 "neurodivergent people may be at heightened risk of neurodiscrimination. Equally older workers may be disproportionately impacted as a result of age discrimination on the basis of neural data") ✓.

### (e) Futures Lab 2026

The submission names "people of African descent, queer individuals, and other marginalized groups, particularly in policing, employment, and judicial contexts" as those "Biased datasets and predictive systems may disproportionately harm" [Futures Lab 2026, p. 2](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p3 "Biased datasets and predictive systems may disproportionately harm people of African descent, queer individuals, and other marginalized groups, particularly in policing, employment, and judicial contexts") ✓. Two points are its own. Participation: those most affected, "including defendants and prisoners", are "regarded as secondary advisors or “objects” in the regulatory process", and "perspectives from low- and middleincome countries and the Global South are underrepresented" [Futures Lab 2026, p. 3](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p4 "including defendants and prisoners, patients and users, vulnerable groups, and marginalized communities, are regarded as secondary advisors or “objects” in the regulatory process … perspectives from low- and middleincome countries and the Global South are underrepresented") ✓. Inference of identity: "mind-reading" tools "may infer sexual orientation or gender identity based on neural responses to stimuli, raising the possibility of involuntary outing in workplaces, medical contexts, or legal systems" [Futures Lab 2026, p. 7](pin:FuturesLab-2026#p8 "may infer sexual orientation or gender identity based on neural responses to stimuli, raising the possibility of involuntary outing in workplaces, medical contexts, or legal systems") ✓.

### (f) Scholarship

Prainsack and colleagues supply the structural frame, about data generally rather than neurotechnology: "We live in an era of inequalities, many of which are being increased by digital technologies" [Prainsack et al. 2022, p. e773](pin:Prainsack-et-al-2022#p1 "We live in an era of inequalities, many of which are being increased by digital technologies") ✓. Their answer, collective governance, is on [[data-solidarity]].

## Open questions

- Evidence. No document reports a case of neurotechnology-based discrimination in a justice setting; the AHRC's COMPAS precedent (on [[neuroprediction]]) is an algorithm, not a brain measurement. The claims are anticipatory, by analogy with algorithmic bias.
- Proof. Indirect discrimination through "non-representative data sets" needs a burden of proof and access to the data; the library does not say who carries it.
- Grounds. Only Futures Lab names sexual orientation and gender identity; only A/81/351 names "neurological difference". Whether that is a protected ground under article 26 is not addressed. The Advisory Committee's list of groups (A/HRC/57/61, para. 37) is cited by two documents but not yet in the library: ingest [[a-hrc-57-61]].
