# Tensions

The register of open disagreements in the wiki. One `###` entry per contested card (the card itself, with its pins and authority ladder, is on the page named in `card:`), then discrepancies of fact between documents, which need no card: one document is simply out of date or wrong. Status: `open` (the documents split), `leaning` (the weight lies on one side, by the authority ladder), `settled` (the library resolves it). Updated on every Ingest.

## Contested claims

### do-existing-rights-suffice
title: Do existing rights suffice, or are new neurorights needed?
status: leaning
card: [[new-rights-vs-existing-rights]]
poles: existing rights, interpreted and clarified (A/81/351 para. 48; AHRC 2025 p. 30) || new UN standards (Futures Lab 2026 p. 7)
where: [[a-81-351]], [[ahrc-2025]], [[futures-lab-2026]], [[szoszkiewicz-yuste-2025]], [[freedom-of-thought]]
note: no treaty body has spoken; the AHRC asks the Human Rights Committee for a general comment on article 18. A/HRC/57/61 (inbox) will add the Advisory Committee's view.

### moratorium-or-safeguards
title: Suspend neurotechnology in criminal justice, or regulate it with safeguards?
status: open
card: [[moratorium]]
poles: a moratorium until assessed (AHRC 2025 Recommendation 6, p. 70) || prohibit coercive uses, regulate the rest (A/81/351 para. 50 (c), (f)); no deployment until validated (UNODC–INTERPOL 2025 p. 5); monitoring (Futures Lab 2026 p. 7)
where: [[ahrc-2025]], [[a-81-351]], [[unodc-interpol-2025]], [[what-should-states-do-now]]
note: the AHRC's own scope varies: "criminal investigations" (p. 32) against "the criminal justice system" (Recommendation 6, p. 70).

## Discrepancies between documents

### us-state-laws-count
title: How many US states protect neural data by law?
status: open
poles: California, Colorado and Montana (A/81/351 para. 45; Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025 p. 3471) || California and Colorado, "the only states" (Futures Lab 2026 p. 4) || "at least eight" (Futures Lab 2026 p. 8)
where: [[regulatory-landscape]], [[colorado-neural-data-law]], [[california-neural-data-law]], [[futures-lab-2026]]
note: the library holds none of these laws; the submission contradicts itself.

### unesco-recommendation-status
title: Is the UNESCO Recommendation adopted or a draft?
status: settled
poles: adopted in 2025 (A/81/351 para. 46) || a draft under negotiation (Szoszkiewicz & Yuste 2025 p. 3471; AHRC 2025 p. 9; Futures Lab 2026 p. 5)

### the-word-undue
title: How strict is the self-incrimination standard?
status: settled
poles: "direct or indirect physical or undue psychological pressure" (General Comment No. 32 para. 41) || the same without "undue", and called "an absolute guarantee" (A/81/351 para. 32; AHRC 2025 pp. 31, 33)
where: [[self-incrimination]], [[ccpr-c-gc-32]], [[a-81-351]], [[iccpr-art-14-3-g]]
note: quote the Committee's own words; the reports' stricter wording is theirs.

### security-of-person-article
title: Which ICCPR article protects security of the person?
status: settled
poles: article 9 (the treaty; see [[iccpr-art-9]]) || "art 19.1 ICCPR" (AHRC 2025 p. 33)
where: [[ahrc-2025]], [[iccpr-art-9]], [[ccpr-c-gc-34]]
note: an error in the AHRC report; article 19(1) protects holding opinions.
