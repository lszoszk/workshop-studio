Before the first step: why we build

Uploading a document to a chatbot gives you a paraphrase that you cannot cite and cannot check. A small tool built on clean data gives you the exact words with their source (the document symbol and the paragraph number), every time. In the session participants see both, and they leave with a tool of their own.

FOR THE FACILITATORS IN THE ROOM

When something goes wrong

The most frequent problems from our test builds and from the room in Venice (12–13 November 2026, about 30 participants), with what to do. The studio never changes a participant's model by itself, so most fixes are one click by the participant.

  1. "Recorded answers" in the top right corner, and "Edit the plan" is grey. The participant has no seat code, so the studio replays recorded answers. Open the link from the QR code on the seat card again (in Venice, a quarter of the first day's visits started this way).
  2. "The studio is paused by the facilitator." Ask me to resume the studio; it is paused before and after the session.
  3. "The model did not answer. Try again, or use the other model." Press Try again once. If several people see it at the same time, tell me: it is a key or a provider problem, and the other model usually still works.
  4. "The model is busy right now." Too many requests at once. Wait a minute and press Try again, or switch to the other model with the button under the message.
  5. An error in red as the app opens (for instance "Cannot set properties of null"). The studio sends it back to the model once by itself; then press Ask the model to fix it, and after two failed fixes, Load a working version. In Venice almost all such errors came from GLM-5.3 (33 of 93 builds, against 2 of 136 for Claude Sonnet).
  6. The model answers in words, and no app appears. Type: "Please build the app now: the whole app in one html code block." (GLM does this now and then.)
  7. A paragraph in the app repeats or garbles its own text. A bug in the app, not in the data, which is exactly what the test card is for: compare the paragraph with its source and ask for a fix ("Show each paragraph exactly as stored; only highlight the matches").
  8. Two apps give different numbers for the same search (for instance 279 and 286). Each follows its own plan: whether "child" also finds "children", whether quotation marks keep a phrase together. Look at the line "How this is computed" and at the participant's answer to "What counts as a match".
  9. Marks such as [[fn:42]] in the text. Not an error: the footnote numbers as the file stores them.
  10. Someone is stuck on a step. Every step of the bar has Skip, and the recorded planning has Skip to the plan, so nobody misses the build or the wall. In Venice the first app took 6 to 12 minutes with a plan; the short plan should cut that.
  11. The page was reloaded. The brief, the plan and the progress come back in the same browser; another browser or device starts from the beginning.
  12. Safari, or a small laptop screen. Both work (in Venice three visits in four were in Safari, and two thirds of the screens were narrower than 1440 px). If the app is cramped, Close the plan or open the app in a New tab.
