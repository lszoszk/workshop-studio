The plan this app was built from · GLM-5.3

Paragraphs out of a PDF: the numbered paragraphs of a General Comment, from a PDF converter's text, checked against the reference

Context

The user works in an office that quotes UN documents and copies each paragraph out of the PDF by hand, removing the footnote numbers and page headers that come with it. A PDF converter copies in a second but damages the text: it breaks a paragraph across two pages, adds page headers, footers and date stamps, leaves the footnotes at the foot of each page with the paragraph text, and loses some footnote numbers. This app turns the converter's raw text of one document, WORKSHOP_DATA.pdf_text, into numbered paragraphs with the heading each sits under, compares the result with the document's own reference, flags the pages where footnote text may sit inside a paragraph, and lets the office take the paragraphs away as a dataset. Footnotes are not separated in this version. The pages are records (page, text); the yardstick is reference (paragraphs, footnotes, adopted, first_words); title, symbol, doc_url and source describe the document; nothing is typed in by hand.

What I found in the data

These numbers are from the file I examined; the app computes its own.

  • 20 pages, text filled on every page, 2,800 to 4,300 characters each; count is the string "20". reference.paragraphs 90, reference.footnotes 141; reference.first_words keyed by the paragraph number as a string.
  • Header. The first line of every page is the document symbol.
  • Footer. The last lines of a page are, in varying order, the page number alone on a line (pages 2 to 20) and the job code "GE.25-15926"; page 1 has "GE.25-15926 (E)" and a date stamp "051125 061125" instead. On 12 pages a third bare number sits in the footer: a footnote number the converter displaced.
  • Paragraph numbers. 88 of 90 stand alone on their line ("1." then the text on the next line); 2 (paragraphs 23 and 87) share the line with their text. A rule for one form alone misses the other.
  • Headings. Roman-numbered sections ("I.", a blank line, "Introduction") and lettered subsections ("A.") whose title can wrap over two lines; and 20 unlabelled headings ("Right to work (art. 6)", "Children") on the line or two lines just before a paragraph number, so a naive split glues them to the previous paragraph; the one before paragraph 73 wraps over two lines.
  • Footnotes. Each page's footnotes sit at the foot, before the footer: the first is a bare number on its own line, a blank line, then the text; the others start with their number and a space ("7 E/2003/22…"). Every page has footnotes, so the paragraph that runs at the foot of each page carries footnote text unless the footnotes are separated: 20 of 90 paragraphs here. Builds that tried to separate them lost the count after the first missing number, so this version flags them and leaves them in place.
  • Footnote marks in the text. 141 marks, glued to the word before ("waste.2") or after a space ("”. 3"); they are left as found, except in the first-words comparison.
  • 16 paragraphs span a page break; paragraphs are 43 to 254 words of plain prose, no line ends with a hyphen; curly quotes and en dashes occur, so comparisons normalise them.

How every number is computed

The pipeline runs once on load, in this order, over records sorted by page.

  • Header. If a page's first non-empty line equals symbol, drop it; otherwise note a warning for that page.
  • Footer. From the end of each page, drop lines while the line is empty, starts with "GE." (the job code), consists only of 6-digit groups (the date stamp), or is a bare number of one to three digits. A bare number equal to page is the page number; any other is counted as "bare number removed from the footer" (a footnote number the converter displaced; the app does not put it back).
  • Body lines of all pages are concatenated (every line that is not header or footer, footnote lines included) in page order, each line tagged with its page; this joins the paragraphs that cross a page break.
  • Paragraph starts. A line matching /^(\d{1,3})\.$/ (number alone) or /^(\d{1,3})\. (.+)$/ (number and text on one line; the text after the space is the first line). Lines before the first paragraph start are the cover.
  • Headings. A line matching /^([IVX]+|[A-Z])\.$/ opens a labelled heading: skip blank lines, then every line up to the next label line or paragraph start is heading text; a line starting with a lowercase letter or "(" continues the previous heading line. An unlabelled heading is a non-empty line immediately followed by a paragraph-start line, at most 90 characters, starting with a capital letter and not ending with a full stop, semicolon, colon, comma or digit; when the line before it passes the same test and does not end a paragraph sentence, the two lines are one heading. A heading line is never paragraph text. Each paragraph's section is the most recent heading before it and part the most recent roman-numbered heading.
  • Paragraph lines. Every other non-empty body line belongs to the current paragraph; blank lines are dropped. page_start and page_end are the pages of its first and last line.
  • Paragraph text = the paragraph's lines joined with a single space, double spaces collapsed, trimmed. No line in the file ends with a hyphen, so no hyphen joining is done; the app counts such lines anyway and reports the number. text_lines keeps the original lines for the toggle and the export field text_lines (lines separated by " | ").
  • Suspect lines. A line inside a paragraph is suspect when it is a bare number of one to three digits with no full stop after it, or starts with one to three digits, a space and a capital letter, a UN document symbol or a quotation mark ("7 E/2003/22…", "2 Summary for policymakers…"), or starts with an asterisk and a space (" Adopted by the Committee…", the note on the first page). A paragraph start always has a full stop after its number, so the two never mix. A suspect line and every line after it to the end of its page are suspect: a footnote block runs to the foot of the page. A paragraph over 300 words is flagged too (the longest real one here has 254). A paragraph is flagged* when it has a suspect line or is over 300 words, and gets flagged true. On this file the rule flags 20 of 90 paragraphs, one per page: the paragraph that runs at the foot of each page, above its footnotes.
  • Suspect lines stay in the text. They remain part of their paragraph's text and text_raw; the expanded paragraph on tab 02 highlights them.
  • First words. The first six whitespace-separated words of text, after removing footnote marks: digits glued to the end of a word or a punctuation mark are cut ("waste.2" becomes "waste."), and a token of digits alone is dropped only when the token before it ends with a full stop, comma or closing quote and is not an abbreviation such as "No." or "art." (paragraphs 24, 27, 46 and 60 start with real numbers, "article 2" or "No. 3", which must stay); compared with reference.first_words[String(number)], equal after turning curly quotes straight, en and em dashes into a hyphen and runs of whitespace into one space; case is kept.
  • Citation = symbol + ", para. " + the paragraph number, with symbol read from WORKSHOP_DATA.pdf_text, never typed in.
  • Counts are loops in JavaScript; nothing is estimated, no model is called.

How the app checks itself

Shown under Every check on tab 01, closed until opened. One line per check: what was compared, the two values, and "pass" in green or "check failed" in red with a link to the rows concerned. A failed check never hides a result.

  • Paragraph count. Found against reference.paragraphs.
  • Numbering. Exactly 1 to n in order; gaps and duplicates listed by number and page.
  • First words. Mismatches counted; listed under the tiles of tab 01, the row flagged on tab 02.
  • Paragraphs flagged. A flag is not a failure: the paragraph count can be right while footnote text sits inside a paragraph, so this line reads "n paragraphs flagged on n pages, to check against the PDF".
  • Suspect lines. n suspect lines on n pages, in n paragraphs; none is removed.
  • Headers and footers. Pages whose first line was the symbol (expected all); pages with a page number in the footer (expected all but the first); bare numbers removed from the footer, listed with their page.
  • Headings. The number of labelled and unlabelled headings, with the unlabelled ones listed, so a reader can spot a paragraph's last line mistaken for a heading.
  • Nothing lost. Every non-empty line of every page is classified exactly once as cover, header, heading, paragraph or footer, and the counts per kind sum to the total non-empty lines in the file; any unclassified line is listed in red with its page.
  • Export round trip. The JSON to download is parsed back; its record count equals the table's.

Edge cases and risks

  • A paragraph number on the same line as its text (2 of 90 here) and alone on its line (88) must both be accepted; handling one form only loses two paragraphs or finds none.
  • "6 November 2025" on the cover starts with a number and a space; it is never suspect because the cover comes before the first paragraph.
  • A line of real paragraph text may start with a number and a space ("17 Sustainable Development Goals"); it is flagged, which costs a reader one look at the PDF, never a lost line.
  • The reference is a yardstick, not the truth: when the counts disagree, the app shows both and never trims or pads its result to match.
  • If reference or one of its fields is missing, the tiles say "no reference in this file" and the checks that need it show "not run", never "pass"; if no paragraph is found, tab 01 says so and the exports are disabled.

Design and accessibility

What this app adds to the studio look:

  • Red accent on the active tab, the paragraph numbers, the flagged-paragraphs number and the words "first words differ", "may contain footnote text" and "check failed"; "pass" dark green: words as well as colours.
  • Big serif numbers only on the rail and the tiles of tab 01. Every table has a serif <caption> stating the message ("90 paragraphs found, 90 expected"), a monospace number (TAB.01), <th scope="col">, numbers right-aligned.
  • A suspect line in the expanded paragraph is marked by a thin red box.
  • The check panel follows "Quality assurance of code for analysis and research: Testing code" (Analysis Function): each check tells a story, given this data, having run this code, we expect this output.
  • The exports follow The Turing Way's "Documentation and metadata": a dataset travels with its source, its fields and its known limitations.
  • Citations follow the Dag Hammarskjöld Library's "How should I cite a UN document?": fields stored apart and formatted on copy, the symbol as the key, the paragraph as the pinpoint.
  • Keyboard: rows and closed headings are buttons with aria-expanded; a hidden tab panel is not focusable.

Out of scope

No footnote separation: suspect lines are flagged and stay in the text. No model calls, no summaries, no OCR, no PDF rendering: the input is the converter's text. No editing in the browser, one document.
