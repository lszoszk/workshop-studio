The plan this app was built from · Mistral Large 3

Paragraphs out of a UN document: the numbered paragraphs of a General Comment, from its HTML, checked against the reference

Context

The user works in an office that quotes UN documents and copies each paragraph by hand. OHCHR's treaty body database serves the document as HTML, which splits cleanly into paragraphs but has lost things on the way. This app turns WORKSHOP_DATA.html_doc into numbered paragraphs with the heading each sits under, compares the result with the document's own reference, says plainly what this HTML version lacks, and lets the office take the paragraphs away as a dataset. The document is html (a string: parse it with new DOMParser().parseFromString(html, 'text/html')); the yardstick is reference (paragraphs, footnotes, adopted, first_words); title, symbol, doc_url and source describe it; nothing is typed in by hand.

What I found in the data

These numbers are from the file I examined; the app computes its own.

  • One document, 71,380 characters of HTML; reference.paragraphs 90, reference.footnotes 141, reference.first_words keyed by the paragraph number as a string.
  • Elements. A cover <table> (the symbol broken over a <span>), then <div class="content">: a bold <p> with the committee's name, the title as an <h2> ending in " *", 6 roman-numbered <h2>, 24 <h3> (4 lettered, 20 plain, "Right to work (art. 6)") and 90 numbered <p>; 97 <p> in all.
  • Paragraph numbers are glued to the text: all 90 read "1.A clean…", none "1. A clean". Heading labels too, in 8 of 10 ("I.Introduction"; "D. Obligation" has its space).
  • Spaces are missing inside the text. 23 sentence joins ("levels.Key"), 8 comma joins ("land,wetlands"), 2 quote joins ("crises“increase"), 2 dash joins ("challenges –to"): 35 places in 24 paragraphs that a rule can prove. And word pairs no rule can see: "Giventhe", "Partiesto", "11(2)(a)". The first-words check catches 6 paragraphs (13, 18, 19, 30, 41, 60), only in their first six words.
  • Nothing of the footnotes survives: no footnote text, no footnote marks (the PDF has "waste.2", this file "waste."), and the title's asterisk points to a note that is not in the file.
  • Characters a reader does not expect (kept as published; the first-words check normalises them): 8 non-breaking hyphens (U+2011, "business‑related") beside 106 ordinary ones; 26 en dashes; 20 right single quotes; 5 pairs of curly quotes; 2 non-breaking spaces inside headings. Correct typography that a search typed on a keyboard misses.
  • Paragraphs are 42 to 253 words of plain prose.

The app, tab by tab

The arrangement of the workshop's Verbatim finder, with two tabs: a reader who sees such a tool for the first time needs the result and the paragraphs, and everything else stays one click away, closed.

Header, always visible. A monospace label "UN DOCUMENT · HTML", the serif title, one line: symbol linked to doc_url (new tab), "adopted reference.adopted", source. At the right a green dot "n of m checks pass" or a red dot "n checks failed", then "1 source limit" (the footnotes).

Left rail, always visible. Headed "DOCUMENT": a card with symbol in monospace and the short title. Two big serif numbers: "n paragraphs of reference.paragraphs" with a thin bar (red when short or over); "0 footnotes of reference.footnotes" in red, "not in this HTML". Then "n of m checks pass".

Under the header, numbered tabs (role="tablist", arrow keys): 01 RESULT · 02 PARAGRAPHS.

01 RESULT. Did the app find everything the document has? Four tiles, big serif numbers, a "How this is computed" line each: Paragraphs found: n beside the document has reference.paragraphs (green when equal, red with the difference); First words match: n of reference.paragraphs; Footnotes: 0 of reference.footnotes, in red, "this HTML version has none: no footnote text, no footnote marks. To cite a footnote, use the PDF or Word version."; Spaces: n in n paragraphs. When first words differ, the paragraphs concerned are listed under the tiles, one line each, "para. n: found … / reference …" (at most ten lines, then "and n more").

Under them Take the paragraphs away: Two buttons: Download JSON and Download Markdown. The JSON file is an array of records, each with symbol, paragraph (its number), section and part (the nearest heading and the roman-numbered section above it), text, text_raw, first_words_match, citation, doc_url and limitations ("no footnotes in this HTML version; the document has n"). The Markdown file opens with front matter between --- lines (title, symbol, adopted, source_url, extracted: today's date, paragraphs_found, paragraphs_expected, footnotes: the same sentence), then the headings as ## and ### lines in document order and each paragraph as its number in bold, then its text. File names: the symbol with slashes replaced by hyphens; a Blob URL, UTF-8.

Last, two closed headings, buttons with aria-expanded: Every check (m), the check panel described below, one line per check; and The spaces restored (n): Paragraph | Before | After | Rule, the count per rule above it (sentence, comma, quote, dash join); clicking a row opens the paragraph on tab 02. Under it: "Rules restore a space only where the characters prove it was there. Word pairs such as 'Giventhe' are not touched." Nothing else on the tab: at most seven things to click.

02 PARAGRAPHS. What does each paragraph say, and how do I cite it? One row per paragraph: No. | Section | First line | Check. Section is the nearest heading above the paragraph ("Right to work (art. 6)"); the expanded row shows the full path ("IV. … › Right to work (art. 6)"). A closed "Headings found: n" box above the table lists them, so none can hide inside a paragraph. First line: the first 120 characters cut at a word with "…"; Check: "OK" or "first words differ" (red). Rows are buttons with aria-expanded; click or Enter expands the row in place: the full text, verbatim, never shortened; the reference's six words beside the found six; the places where a space was missing in this paragraph, marked; and a Copy citation button that copies "symbol, para. n" ("E/C.12/GC/27, para. 12") and shows "Copied" for two seconds; the same string is the citation field of every export.

Footer, always visible. source, built, and "Done by this page's code; no model was called and nothing left the browser."

How every number is computed

The pipeline runs once on load, over the parsed document's body elements in order.

  • Classification. Walk every table, p, h2 and h3 element in document order (doc.body.querySelectorAll('table, p, h2, h3')), not only the body's children: everything after the cover sits inside <div class="content">. Elements inside the first <table> are cover. After it, a <p> whose text matches /^(\d{1,3})\.\s*(.*)$/s is a paragraph with that number; the first <h2> is the title; every other <h2> and <h3> is a heading, split by /^([IVX]+|[A-Z])\.\s*(.+)$/ into label and heading when it matches; any other <p> is the committee line.
  • Paragraph text. text_raw = the text after the number and full stop, trimmed, runs of whitespace collapsed to one space.
  • Repairs. text = text_raw with a space inserted after a full stop between a lowercase and a capital letter (/([a-z])\.([A-Z])/g → "$1. $2"), after a comma between letters (/([a-z]),([A-Za-z])/g), before an opening curly quote touching a letter (/([a-z])“/g) and after an en dash touching a letter (/–([A-Za-z])/g). Each insertion is logged with the paragraph, the rule and 20 characters each side; nothing else changes.
  • Section = the nearest heading above the paragraph; part = the nearest roman-numbered <h2>. A heading is never paragraph text.
  • First words. The first six whitespace-separated words of text against reference.first_words[String(number)]: equal after turning curly quotes straight, en dashes and non-breaking hyphens into a hyphen and non-breaking spaces into spaces; case is kept.
  • Citation = symbol + ", para. " + the paragraph number, with symbol read from WORKSHOP_DATA.html_doc, never typed in.
  • Counts are loops in JavaScript; nothing is estimated, no model is called.

How the app checks itself

Shown under Every check on tab 01, closed until opened. One line per check: what was compared, the two values, and "pass" in green, "check failed" in red with a link to the rows concerned, or "source limit" in red for what the source cannot give. A failed check never hides a result.

  • Paragraph count. Found against reference.paragraphs.
  • Numbering. Exactly 1 to n in order; gaps and duplicates listed.
  • First words. Mismatches counted; listed under the tiles of tab 01, the row flagged on tab 02.
  • Footnotes. 0 against reference.footnotes: "source limit", with "the HTML version has no footnotes: a limit of the source, not of the app", counted apart from failures.
  • Repairs. n spaces restored in n paragraphs, counted per rule; every one is listed under its closed heading on tab 01, and text_raw still holds the text as found.
  • Headings. How many <h2> and <h3>, and how many glued labels were separated.
  • Nothing lost. Every block element is classified exactly once and the counts per class sum to the total; any unclassified element is listed in red.
  • Export round trip. The JSON to download is parsed back; its record count equals the table's.

Edge cases and risks

  • A number glued to its text and one followed by a space are both accepted (\s*): this file has 90 of the first, none of the second; another file may be the reverse.
  • A rule may insert a space only where the characters prove one was there. "Giventhe" stays, caught only when it falls in a paragraph's first six words; a reader who quotes a paragraph still reads it.
  • The reference is a yardstick, not the truth: when the counts disagree, the app shows both and never trims or pads its result to match.
  • If reference or one of its fields is missing, the tiles say "no reference in this file" and the checks that need it show "not run", never "pass"; if html yields no <p>, tab 01 says so and the exports are disabled.

Design and accessibility

What this app adds to the studio look:

  • Red accent on the active tab, the paragraph numbers, the footnotes tile and the words "first words differ", "check failed" and "source limit"; "pass" dark green: words as well as colours.
  • Big serif numbers only on the rail and the tiles of tab 01. Every table has a serif <caption> stating the message ("90 paragraphs found, 90 expected"), a monospace number (TAB.01), <th scope="col">, numbers right-aligned.
  • A repaired or flagged place is marked in the expanded paragraph by a thin red box.
  • The check panel follows "Quality assurance of code for analysis and research: Testing code" (Analysis Function): each check tells a story, given this data, having run this code, we expect this output; every damage found has a named check.
  • The exports follow The Turing Way's "Documentation and metadata": a dataset travels with its source, its fields and its known limitations.
  • Citations follow the Dag Hammarskjöld Library's "How should I cite a UN document?": fields stored apart and formatted on copy, the symbol as the key, the paragraph as the pinpoint.
  • Keyboard: rows and closed headings are buttons with aria-expanded; a hidden tab panel is not focusable.

Out of scope

No footnote extraction: the source has none. No model calls, no summaries, no comparison with the PDF text, no editing in the browser, one document.
