Verbatim finder: exact paragraphs of General Comments, with a citation to paste

Context

The user drafts submissions: a shadow report, an amicus brief, a statement to a treaty body. The reader will check every quotation against the General Comment, so a paraphrase or a wrong paragraph number costs credibility. Today the words come from OHCHR's site or a PDF, copied by hand, or from a chatbot that invents paragraph numbers that sound right. This app searches WORKSHOP_DATA.gc, the treaty bodies' General Comments and General Recommendations paragraph by paragraph, with Boolean operators and exact phrases; shows the matching paragraphs as stored; copies a citation built only from the record's fields; and collects quotations for the draft. It retrieves; it never generates. No model is called and nothing leaves the browser.

What I found in the data

Numbers are from the file I examined; the app computes its own from records at load.

  • 7,304 records, every field filled except cited_articles (2,398). records.length is the count; the top-level count is the string "7304".
  • A symbol is not a document: 159 distinct symbol, 188 distinct title. 28 symbols are annual reports (A/48/18) or pages of HRI/GEN/1/Rev.9, and 14 hold more than one General Comment; A/48/18 holds seven, so "A/48/18, para. 3" alone is ambiguous.
  • Three joint General Comments carry two symbols in one symbol ("CMW/C/GC/7–CERD/C/GC/38", en dash; "CRC/C/GC/23, CMW/C/GC/4", comma) but one committee.
  • paragraph runs 0 to 135. 33 records have paragraph 0: preambles of early CEDAW and CERD General Recommendations. 22 records are lettered sub-paragraphs ("(a) To take note…") that the file numbers 1, 2, 3.
  • 4,258 texts begin with their own number ("12. The Committee…"), 3,046 do not; in 5 the number differs from paragraph (three in CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.5, numbered 3.1, 3.2 in the original). Two pinpoints occur twice (CEDAW/C/GC/27 para. 9; the joint GR 31/GC 18 para. 31).
  • Footnote marks are written [[fn:N]]: 3,238 in 1,857 paragraphs, 212 between two words of a sentence, so the string "Education For All. By virtue" does not exist although the sentence does.
  • Quotes are curly: 3,169 curly double quotes against 82 straight; 2,938 curly apostrophes against 78 straight; 248 en dashes, 107 non-breaking spaces; "non-State" 145 times.
  • 40 paragraphs contain line breaks; 208 end with a colon and their list sits in the following records. Length 4 to 1,287 words. 10 committee codes (CRC 2,091 … CED 56; CAT-OP is the Subcommittee on Prevention of Torture); year 1972–2026. cited_articles is a third filled and 131 entries start with "?": not offered as a filter.

How every number is computed

  • Load. R = WORKSHOP_DATA.gc.records; totals are R.length, the sets of title and committee, the min and max of year. Per record, once: norm, marks, leading (^(\d{1,3})\.\s on text), pinpoint, citation, shared (the symbol holds more than one title), joint, committees.
  • Normalised text norm, once per record at load = text with [[fn:N]] removed; “ ” „ → "; ‘ ’ ‚ → '; en dash, em dash and soft hyphen → "-"; non-breaking space and all other whitespace, line breaks included, → one space; lower case. The query is normalised the same way, so curly quotes pasted from Word still make a phrase.
  • Query parser. Tokens: a phrase in double quotes (straight or curly); ( ); AND, OR, NOT in capitals; a leading - as NOT; any other run of non-space characters as a word, trailing * allowed. Adjacent terms are joined by AND. Precedence NOT, AND, OR; parentheses override. The parser returns a tree and an English echo, or the error shown in the box.
  • Term match. A word becomes a regular expression over norm: the word escaped, trailing * → \w*, boundaries that work for accented letters, (?<![\p{L}\p{N}]) before and (?![\p{L}\p{N}]) after, flag u. A hyphen is a boundary, so "state" finds "non-State". A phrase is its words joined by \s+ inside the same boundaries. A record matches when the Boolean tree is true over norm; a NOT-only query matches everything that lacks the terms, and the echo says so.
  • Highlight. The same terms become a second regular expression over the shown text, where a quote or apostrophe matches its straight and curly forms, a hyphen matches hyphen, en and em dash, and a gap between phrase words matches (?:\s|\[\[fn:\d+\]\])+; matches are wrapped in <mark> after HTML-escaping. NOT terms are never highlighted.
  • Hits = records whose tree matches and that pass the committee, year and document filters. Hits per committee = one count per committee over the hits, by each record's committee:
  • A record's committees = the codes found in symbol (\b(CAT|CCPR|CED|CEDAW|CERD|CESCR|CMW|CRC|CRPD)\b, "CAT-OP" for CAT/OP) plus committee; the filter keeps a record if any is ticked, and the chart adds it to each of its committees' bars.
  • Shown and copied text = text with every [[fn:N]] removed (\[\[fn:\d+\]\], global); marks = the number removed. Line breaks, non-breaking spaces and curly quotes stay.
  • Leading number. When leading equals paragraph, the copied quotation drops the "12. " at the start (the citation carries the number); the shown text keeps it. When they differ, nothing is dropped and the row carries the amber note.
  • Citation = symbol + ", " + pinpoint. Pinpoint: "para. " + paragraph; "preamble" when paragraph is 0; "subpara. (b)" when text starts with a lettered label (^\(([a-z]{1,4})\)). When the symbol is shared by more than one title (computed at load), prefix committee + ", " + the document's name (title up to the first colon or " on ") and put the symbol in parentheses.
  • Copy uses navigator.clipboard.writeText; if refused, the text goes into a selectable box with "Select and copy". After every copy an aria-live message shows the exact string sent.
  • Order = year descending, then title, then paragraph ascending. The select swaps the comparator; the hit set does not change.

How the app checks itself

Each check is one line: what was compared, the values, "pass" in green or "check failed" in red with the pinpoints concerned. A failed check marks rows; it never drops them.

  • Query understood. The English echo and the number of terms; a parse error appears here too.
  • Totals. Rows listed across pages equal the hits; the checkboxes' bracketed counts sum to the hits plus joint hits.
  • Chart total. The bars sum to the hits plus the joint hits, both shown.
  • Highlight. Every hit shows at least one <mark> unless the query is NOT-only; a hit matched in norm but without a highlight is listed as "matched, not highlighted" and still shown.
  • Normalisation. When the query holds a straight quote, apostrophe or hyphen, the panel says the curly and dash forms were matched too, and how many hits contain only those forms.
  • Numbering. Hits whose leading number differs from paragraph, with both numbers; hits that share a pinpoint with another record of the same title; hits that are lettered sub-paragraphs.
  • Marks. For every hit, the marks removed equal the count of [[fn: in the stored text, and putting them back gives the stored text exactly.
  • Draft. For every item, the stored text of the record with that id is looked up again and compared with the item's text_as_stored; a difference marks the item.
  • Known quirks of this file, computed at load and shown before any search: records with paragraph 0, symbols shared by several documents, joint documents, duplicated pinpoints, leading-number mismatches, each with its count and an example.

Edge cases and risks

  • The app can only return paragraphs that exist, with their real symbol and paragraph; citation and quotation are built from fields, and the Check & method tab says so.
  • Preambles are cited as "preamble", never "para. 0"; lettered sub-paragraph records as "subpara. (b)" with a reminder to verify; shared symbols carry the General Recommendation's number, or a reader lands in a 200-page report.
  • A leading number that differs from paragraph is never corrected: the row shows both.
  • Older General Comments may be replaced by later ones (Human Rights Committee GC 36 replaced GC 6 and 14); the file has no "replaced" field, so year is on every row.
  • Whole words: "child" does not find "children"; the syntax line says to type child*. Lower-case "or" is a word.
  • Long paragraphs are cut on screen only; copy and draft take the whole text. Clipboard or localStorage can be refused in a sandbox: the fallback box and memory are always there.
  • An empty query with filters lists every paragraph that passes them, paged, and the summary says "no search words".

Design and accessibility

On top of the studio look:

  • Red accent on the active tab, the ticked committees and filter chips, the pinpoints ("para. 18") and the bars of FIG.01. Other colours: the <mark> highlight (also bold), amber for numbering notes, green for "pass", each also a word.
  • Big numbers: the matching count in the rail (40–56 px serif). Committee badges are plain outlined monospace labels, one colour.
  • Figure title: "FIG.01 Paragraphs per committee".
  • The chart follows "Data visualisation: charts (formatting, labels, bar charts)" (UK Government Analysis Function): gaps narrower than a bar, at most ten light gridlines, horizontal labels.
  • The canvas follows "Chart.js: Accessibility of canvas charts": role="img", an aria-label that states the numbers, fallback content, and a visible table with the same values.
  • The syntax follows "Wikipedia Help:Searching": double quotes for a phrase, a leading hyphen for exclusion, capital OR, a star for endings; the help text is written fresh, since that page is CC BY-SA.
  • Citations follow "Dag Hammarskjöld Library, Ask DAG: how should I cite a UN document?": parts stored separately and formatted on copy, the symbol as the key, a pinpoint always, the style named on screen, no symbol or URL ever invented.
  • Keyboard: the search box has focus on load; the example list opens with the arrow keys; rows are <article>s with a heading; the count, the summary line and the "Copied" message are aria-live="polite"; the chart's click is duplicated by the checkboxes.

Out of scope

No model calls, summaries or "similar paragraphs". No stemming or synonyms beyond the star. No tracking of which General Comment replaced which. No filter on cited_articles. No concluding observations or views, no languages other than English, no editing of texts.
